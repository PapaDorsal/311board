# 311board — Design Brief

Status: step 0 is complete and the verdict is folded in below. The premise
SURVIVES. Live evidence: `tools/311-findings.md` and `tools/311-findings-b.md`,
regenerated by `tools/spike-311.mjs` / `tools/spike-311b.mjs`. Everything here
is direction, not a commitment to ship.

## The thing we're making

A Chicago ward-level 311 response-time leaderboard. Public 311 service requests
are already open data; what nobody publishes is the comparison — which wards
close which request types fast, and which don't. The product is that
comparison, made legible.

## Register

The through-line across the earlier builds worth carrying here: **take the
bureaucratic artifact seriously as data, refuse to take its language
seriously.**

That is the whole voice. Concretely:

- The underlying record is treated with real rigor — correct fields, honest
  aggregation, stated methodology, no massaged numbers.
- The *presentation* of that record is plain-spoken and a little deadpan.
  "Pothole in the public way" becomes "potholes." A 47-day median becomes "a
  month and a half."
- Never sneering at the people inside the system. The joke is the language and
  the process, not the ward superintendent. This is what makes it land with
  residents and with the bureaucrats simultaneously — a bureaucrat should be
  able to send it to a colleague without it reading as an attack.
- No triumphalism, no outrage-farming. Deadpan means the number carries the
  weight; we don't add adjectives to it.

## Principles

1. **Lead with a live, concrete hook.** Not "explore 311 data." A specific,
   currently-true sentence: "Ward 44 takes three weeks to close an abandoned
   vehicle complaint. Ward 11 takes one." The hook is generated from the data
   and changes as the data changes. Step 0 taught us the hook's *shape*: state
   the absolute gap in days, not a ratio — when the fast ward's median is near
   zero, a ratio inflates to a number nobody should print (we caught our own
   spike reporting a 4935× "gap" that was really 0.97 days over ~0).
2. **Rank with receipts.** Every ward's position is backed by the actual
   requests behind it — real request text, real dates, real durations. A score
   with no visible source is a rumor. Any number on the page should be one
   click from the rows that produced it.
3. **Methodology in view, not in a footer.** How we define "closed," what we do
   with reopened and duplicate requests, which types are excluded and why. In
   the page, at the point of the claim. Being visibly honest about limits is
   what earns the right to make the comparison at all.
4. **Plain language over official language,** with the official term available.
   Show "graffiti removal," keep "Graffiti Removal Request" one hover away, so
   anyone checking our work can match it against the source.
5. **Comparison is the product.** A single ward's number means little. Rank,
   spread, and the fastest/slowest gap are the substance.

## Visual direction

Prior art in this space (chispections and similar) is all-dark terminal mono.
That's a *reference for attitude, not a template* — the attitude is right, the
execution is a ceiling we should clear.

- Lighter, more legible visual system. Real typographic hierarchy rather than
  one monospace weight doing every job.
- Monospace stays, but as an accent for data — figures, durations, IDs — not as
  body text.
- Density is fine; illegibility is not. This is a table-forward product and the
  tables should be genuinely pleasant to read.
- Light and dark both work properly. Not a dark-mode gimmick.

## Step-0 verdict (2025 data, live queries)

The three questions this brief left open, answered:

- **Does the premise survive?** Yes. 2025 closure rate is 98.17%
  (1,924,710 of 1,960,595 rows), and per-ward spread on a well-chosen type is
  large enough to carry a ranking: 16.1 days end to end.
- **Can we rank all 50 wards?** Show all 50, but draw headline
  fastest/slowest only from wards with n ≥ 200 for the chosen type (47 of 50
  for abandoned vehicles). Thin wards stay in the table, flagged — excluded
  from nothing, trusted for less. The extremes of a leaderboard are where
  readers look first and where small-n percentiles are least stable; our own
  spike's "fastest ward" moved by 5 days when a 183-row ward was set aside.
- **Which type carries the front page?** **Abandoned Vehicle Complaint.**
  52,937 rows in 2025, all 50 wards, p50 6.7 days (ward 11) to 22.8 days
  (ward 44), zero same-second closures, zero cancellations, no facility-address
  concentration (top ward holds 3.7%). The obvious alternative fails: Graffiti
  Removal is the highest-volume actionable type but closes in under a day
  citywide with three wards' medians pinned at 0.00 by what looks like bulk
  administrative closure — no legible story there.

What the data forced us to learn, now standing methodology (principle 3 makes
us say this on the page, not just here):

- **Absolute gaps in days are the headline; ratios only when the fast median
  clears a floor** (we use 0.05 days). See principle 1.
- **"Closed" means status Completed.** Canceled requests are administrative
  closes, not service delivered — 789 of Garbage Cart Maintenance's 2025
  "closed" rows were cancellations. Excluded, and the exclusion stated.
- **Facility-stamped types are excluded from any per-ward volume figure.**
  662,321 informational calls are logged at the 311 call center's own address
  (ward 28) and 364,900 aircraft-noise complaints at O'Hare (ward 41); raw
  volume makes those wards look like they carry ~30× normal demand. The spike
  now flags any type with >50% single-ward concentration automatically.
- **The duplicate flag is real and nonzero** (2.84% of garbage-cart rows) and
  needs an explicit, stated policy before launch.

What this bought the register: every deadpan sentence on the page can now be
backed by a number we have actually pulled, and the methodology we promised to
keep "in view, not in a footer" already exists as running code.
