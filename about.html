About

ChiWardBoard ranks Chicago’s 50 wards by how fast the city closes 311 service requests filed in them - towing an abandoned car, filling a pothole, baiting an alley. The records are public. The comparison was not. Now it is.

The numbers come from the City of Chicago’s 311 Service Requests dataset. Nothing here is our own measurement. Anyone can check the figures against the source.

A ward that closes requests quickly is not necessarily a better-run ward. Some of these gaps come from how much arterial street a ward has, or how many people file requests in the first place. The methodology below says what these numbers can and cannot tell you.

Every ward page carries that ward office’s published contact information, along with a link to file a 311 request yourself, because the numbers only help if you know who to call.

Not affiliated with the City of Chicago. Free and open to anyone, with no account and no ads. Corrections welcome at after.hours.builder@gmail.com.

Methodology

  • Period. The board defaults to a rolling 12 months ending at the most recent complete month, so a part-filled month never distorts a figure; the city’s own published median dataset uses the same shape. Calendar years 2024 and 2025 can also be selected. Earlier years are not offered: the ward map changed in May 2023, and comparing across it would compare different areas under the same ward numbers.
  • Who represents a ward. The alderperson named on a ward page is that ward’s current representative, taken from the city’s Ward Offices directory, as are the contact details on that page. Where the directory carries a link that does not work - a dead domain, or an email address miskeyed into the website field - we correct or drop it, and the build prints a warning when another one stops resolving. Everything else is published exactly as the city has it. The period covered may span more than one officeholder, so a ward’s figures are not necessarily the work of the person named.
  • Source. The City of Chicago’s public 311 Service Requests dataset and the city’s 2023 ward boundaries, plus its traffic crash and bike route datasets for the cycling context on ward pages. Numbers are computed from the rows themselves at build time and published as a dated snapshot; the exact snapshot behind this page is readable here, and every ward page links to the live rows behind its figures.
  • “Closed” means Completed. Only a request the city marks Completed counts as finished work. Cancelled and still-open requests are not thrown away: each one counts as a wait of at least that long. If they were dropped, a ward’s unfinished work would disappear from its own figures and make it look faster than it is.
  • Duplicates are excluded. A report the city flags as a duplicate is the same physical problem reported twice; counting it would inflate the volume and time one repair as if it were two. The city excludes them in its own tooling. The per-type count excluded is shown with each board.
  • Days to close. For a finished request it is the closed timestamp minus the created timestamp. For one still open it is the time waited so far, measured to the moment the data was pulled, and counted as a wait of at least that long. A cancelled request is counted the same way, up to its close. The typical time is estimated from all of them together, so the unfinished ones pull it in the direction they belong. Negative durations and rows with no ward are dropped, and the counts of everything dropped are printed with the claim they affect.
  • Ranking floor. Headline fastest/slowest and rank positions come only from wards with at least 200 completed requests of that type, and only where enough of them closed for a typical time to exist at all. Wards that miss either test are shown and flagged, but not ranked.
  • One board is measured the other way round. Almost every request type here closes eventually and differs only in speed: over two years, the share of potholes, rat complaints or tree debris still open six months on is effectively zero. Sidewalk repairs are the exception in the entire dataset. The city completes so few of them that a speed ranking would rest on a couple of dozen requests per ward, while about a third sit unfinished citywide. So that board ranks wards on the share still open rather than on days to close, over a two-year window, counting only requests filed at least 180 days ago - a request from last month is new, not late. It is the same question with the answer inverted, and it is labelled as such wherever it appears.
  • What an open request does and does not prove. The records carry a status, not an intention. A sidewalk request left open may mean nobody came, or it may mean the city keeps the case open while the repair waits its turn in a capital programme. We publish it as work the city has not signed off on, and say no more than that.
  • Individual requests, and only on city property. Nobody came for these lists specific 311 requests still open more than a year, with the address and the case number, oldest first and with nothing chosen for being interesting. Every type on that list is a complaint about something the city owns and maintains - a street light, a sidewalk, a pothole, a stop sign - so naming the address names a place. Complaints filed against private property and its owners are deliberately never listed there, however long they have been open. The site also records which requests it finds still open at each refresh, so a request can be shown as having sat through several checks months apart; that count starts when the watching started, not when the request was filed.
  • Cycling figures are context, not a ranking. Ward pages carry the number of crashes involving someone on a bike, how many left a person seriously hurt or killed, and how many miles of bike route the ward has - from the city's traffic crash and bike route datasets, placed into wards by their coordinates. These are never ranked. A ward with more crashes is usually a ward with more cycling, and correcting for that needs ridership figures nobody publishes. Reported bike lane obstructions are not here at all: the city logs about 630 a year across all 50 wards, too few to say anything about any one of them.
  • Gaps over ratios. Ward differences are stated in absolute days. When the fastest ward’s typical time is near zero, a ratio divides by almost nothing and becomes meaninglessly large, so we do not print one.
  • What’s off the board. Informational calls and aircraft noise complaints are logged at city facilities (the 311 call center in Ward 28; O’Hare in Ward 41), so they describe a building, not a neighborhood. Types that close almost instantly citywide stay on the board with that fact stated.

Privacy

  • No accounts and no ads, and the site sets no cookies at all.
  • “Use my location” is resolved against the ward map inside your browser. Your coordinates are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Address lookup works the same way. The site downloads a block-to-ward index built from public records, and the match happens on your device. The address you type is never sent to us or to anyone else.
  • The page loads Vercel Web Analytics, which counts visits without cookies and cannot recognise you across days or across other sites. Our hosting provider (Vercel) and Google Fonts also receive standard web requests (IP address, user agent) to serve the page, as with any website.
